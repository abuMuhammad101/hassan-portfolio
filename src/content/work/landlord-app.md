## The brief

Maintenance requests were scattered across phone calls and text messages — no one could reliably track status, hold a record of completed work, or coordinate between tenants, landlords, and service providers. The app needed to become the single place all of that lived.

## What I built

A mobile atomic design system: type scale, spacing, and color tokens, form components with real validation, status indicators and step/progress patterns, and empty states designed with the same care as the happy path.

On top of that, the core end-to-end flows: submit a maintenance request → track the job → mark it complete; manage tenants and properties; review job history and payments in one place. Every flow went through an interactive prototype before it was called done.

## Key decisions

Clear status visibility at every stage — requested, scheduled, in progress, completed — so nobody has to ask "where is this?" A record-first approach, since history that's hard to find is history that doesn't help anyone. Consistent component patterns throughout, so the app stays learnable as it grows.
